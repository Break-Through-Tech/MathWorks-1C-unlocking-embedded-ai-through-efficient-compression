The contending features:
mean value, standard deviation, skewness, kurtosis, second derivative, crest factor, RMS

Ideally we want to avoid reundancies, or features with low predictive power.

Because we are using a population standard deviation:
RMS ** 2 = mean ** 2 + std ** 2.
This means RMS is redundant with mean and std.
Since Crest factor = peak/RMS
Peak is susceptible to various sensitivities (outlier, measurement), we will not include it.
This makes Crest Factor is partially redundant with RMS.
However, since RMS already retains a measure of mean and std, we'll use RMS to retain a measure of Crest factor as well.

(Imagine these are is a waveforms at a certain frequency, plotted over some time domain)

Skewness cannot distinguish
 | | |
-|-|-|-
 | | |
from 
 |    |
-|----|-
 |    |
They both have skewness 0.
It's possible the geometry from the bearing suggests:
Waveform1 is a fault, Waveform2 is not a fault.
Meaning skewness(Waveform1) == skewness(Waveform2).
But their pred(Waveform1) != pred(Waveform2).
This same idea can be generalized to waveforms with similar amplitude distributions,
as they obtain no meaningful information from a measure of skewness.

That argument is why I'm not using skewness as a feature.
